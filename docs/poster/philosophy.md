# Lantern Cartography

A movement for maps of what is watched. The page is a night survey: a field of deep canal ink
with a fine draughtsman's grid, and on it the only light that matters, the light of someone
looking. Each work is meticulously crafted as if it were surveyed by hand over many nights,
the product of deep expertise in drawing what cannot be seen: attention itself.

Space is divided by sight, not by walls. Wedges of warm lamplight fan out from small sources,
overlapping and leaving dark channels between them. Those channels are the real subject: the
negative space where a careful body could pass. Composition is built on that tension, wide lit
fans against thin, exact paths of shadow, and every edge is placed with painstaking attention so
the eye can follow a route without one word of explanation.

Colour is rationed like oil. Ink teal for the ground, a single amber for vigilance that reddens
only where it is most intense, one cool violet for mechanical eyes, and a pale moth-wing blue for
the one figure who must not be seen. Gold appears once, small, and everything in the composition
leans toward it. The palette is calibrated so that safety, danger and desire read at a glance
from across a room, and is the result of master-level restraint.

Marks accumulate patiently: hatching for shadow, dotted patrol loops, sweep arcs measured in
even ticks, survey numbers at the margins. Repetition is the method; nothing is decorative.
Each repeated element is drawn with the steadiness of a hand that has done it ten thousand
times, so that the density rewards long looking.

Text is a whisper in the margin: a name, a sheet number, a scale. Type is thin and spaced like
an engraver's annotation, never louder than the light it labels. The finished sheet should feel
like an artifact from a guild that charts the habits of watchmen: clinical, beautiful, and
quietly thrilling, labored over until nothing could be moved.
